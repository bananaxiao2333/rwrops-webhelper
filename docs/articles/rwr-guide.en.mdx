## Main Game Content

The game is a 2.5D top‑down shooter. Players take on the role of a soldier, fighting alongside other players and allied AI to complete the victory objectives of each map. The game has PVE and PVP modes; **this tutorial covers the PVE mode**.

The content is extensive, so please refer to the table of contents and read patiently. **It is recommended to view this tutorial at 150% zoom** (adjust zoom in the bottom right of the Word document).

## 2. Basic Concepts

### 2.1 Experience Points and Resource Points

#### ① Experience Points (XP)
Soldiers level up by accumulating XP, which increases their rank. Many weapons and radio support options are rank‑restricted; you must reach the required rank to use them.

**Ways to gain XP**:
- Killing enemy soldiers
- Destroying enemy vehicles / deployable weapons / buildings
- Repairing vehicles / deployable weapons / buildings
- Capturing enemy bases
- Reviving downed allies

#### ② Resource Points (RP)
The game's sole currency is **Resource Points (RP)**. RP can be used to buy items, call in support, trade items between players, etc.

**Ways to gain RP**:
- Destroying enemy vehicles and buildings
- Repairing allied vehicles (must have allies inside)
- Capturing enemy bases
- Reviving downed allies
- Selling items directly
- Trading items between players

#### ① Buying and Selling
You can buy and sell equipment at **Armories** and **Armory Vehicles**, as well as at **Supply Vehicles** and **Ambulances**. The **Ice Cream Truck** also allows buying lottery tickets and trading items.

> **⚠️ Important**: Before selling an item, **make sure it is yours**, not something you picked up from another player!

**Player‑to‑player trading** is usually done using **「Dollars」** and **「Gold Bars」** purchased from the armory as equivalent currency. The standard trading prices can be checked in the official domestic trading group; some item prices fluctuate, so consult veteran players first.

> **🚫 Prohibited**: Using **real currency** to trade in‑game items (so‑called "buying gold") is forbidden and may lead to a ban!

#### ② Storing Items
Your **backpack** can temporarily store items. The **Stash** on the map allows you to store and retrieve equipment.

> **Note**: You can only access the stash **when standing at its entrance**.

The backpack has a weight limit. The more items you carry, the heavier your load. **Starting from 20% weight, negative effects begin**, increasing with more weight:

- Reduced movement speed
- Reduced weapon accuracy
- Reduced weapon lethality
- Slower recoil recovery

(Weapon lethality, accuracy, and recoil recovery are explained in Chapter 7‑2‑①/②/④)

Both backpack and stash have item limits:
- Backpack: 255 items
- Stash: 300 items
- Items beyond the limit cannot be stored

Buying the veteran pack can expand stash capacity to 500 items, **but not recommended for new players** – you likely won't need it.

### 2.3 Friendly Fire, Hostile Damage, and Neutral Damage

#### ① Friendly Fire (TK)
Friendly fire is damage caused by any allied weapon / vehicle crushing / explosions, etc. **When friendly fire kills an ally, the dead soldier's equipment does not drop**.

Players should **avoid friendly fire** as much as possible; when it happens, apologise in chat (`sorry`, `sry`, etc.).

> **Special note**: Because friendly fire prevents item drops, experienced players may deliberately TK an ally when they cannot be revived, to save their equipment. Please recognise this and don't blame your teammates wrongly.

> **Note**: Self‑inflicted damage is not friendly fire; **your own items will drop**, so be careful!

#### ② Hostile and Neutral Damage
Both types cause items to drop.
- **Hostile damage**: e.g., killing an enemy – they drop equipment.
- **Neutral damage**: e.g., someone explodes a fuel barrel, killing nearby soldiers – they also drop equipment.

If a soldier is downed and then suicides or bleeds out, **their items will also drop**.

> **⚠️ Beware**: Neutral damage can sometimes be abused to kill allies and steal their equipment. Please report such behaviour with evidence. Common malicious acts include: **vehicle TK**, **offline artillery**, etc.

### 2.4 Spawn Points on the Map

**Yellow dots** on the map are spawn points. After death, open the map and left‑click a spawn point to respawn. Press **Spacebar** after death to quickly respawn at the nearest spawn point to your death location.

> **Note**: If enemies are near a spawn point, it becomes suppressed and cannot be used. **Avoid placing spawn points too close to enemies**.

Spawn points can be created by:
- Existing bases
- Throwing a respawn flare
- Respawn Vehicle
- LAILV‑002 Armored Personnel Carrier (same as Respawn Vehicle)
- Patrol Boat

<img src={images.img_015} style="max-width:100%;height:auto;" />

> **Map spawn points**

### 2.5 Advice for Entering Multiplayer Servers

#### ① Know Yourself and the Enemy
First, **play singleplayer to familiarise yourself with maps, gameplay, and items, and complete a full playthrough before joining multiplayer**. Only when you have a basic understanding will you avoid being clueless and making mistakes. **Ask veteran players for advice, be humble, and don't be stubborn**.

#### ② Step by Step
Start with **basic weapons and items**, gradually learn maps and combat. Don't rush or overreach. Without experience, doing the wrong thing can hurt your team!

#### ③ Help Each Other
When allies need healing or vehicle repairs, help them – they will help you in return. But **always ensure your own safety and surroundings** before assisting.

> **Recommendation**: In multiplayer, players usually move in large groups. **New and low‑level players should follow the main group** and avoid going alone or in pairs.

### 2.6 Additional Notes

During certain holidays (Western festivals), special events occur, featuring unique enemies and event‑specific rewards. Ask in official groups or check Steam news for details.

### 3.2 Maps and Objectives

#### ① Standard Maps – Capture All Enemy Bases
Capture all enemy bases to win. Plan your attack routes to avoid stalemates. If all your bases are lost, the game fails and the map restarts.

<img src={images.img_019} style="max-width:100%;height:auto;" />

> **Standard map**

#### ② Defence Maps – Capture the Central Base and Hold Until Timer Ends
These include `map7`, `map13‑2`, and `map8`. You must capture the central base and hold it until the countdown ends. Place support vehicles wisely (not too far forward) and set up defences around the central base. **There is no failure condition** – you can retry indefinitely.

> **Special note**: On `map13‑2`, there are White faction forces in the centre, hostile to all.

> **⚠️ Critical timing**: On all defence maps, when the countdown reaches **1 minute 10 seconds left**, all enemies will charge **ignoring cover and suppression**, and will remain in attack mode even if they retake the base, until the timer runs out.  
> **Advice**: Prepare defences and take cover before the 1:10 mark; try not to lose the base during the final phase, and avoid attrition battles.

<img src={images.img_020} style="max-width:100%;height:auto;" />

> **Defence map (map13‑2 shown)**

#### ③ Stealth/Special Ops Map – Destroy All Enemy Signal Towers
Only `map11`. First capture a forward base, then infiltrate each enemy base and destroy all signal towers. Victory when the last tower is destroyed. If all your bases are lost, you fail, but enemies do not actively attack your bases.

> **Special note**: This map requires **flexible placement of respawn and supply vehicles** – do not drive them too close to the front line, or they will be suppressed. **New and low‑level players should not drive support vehicles; leave it to experienced players**.

**Normal approach**: Stay hidden, use silenced weapons, infiltrate bases, eliminate enemies, then destroy towers. **Do not make noise** – any alert will draw many reinforcements and ruin the mission. New players should **use only silenced weapons and stealth gear**, support veteran players, and avoid rushing.

**Alternative approach**: When many players are present, you can assault directly with heavy firepower. This requires strong team coordination and firepower, and careful support placement.  
> **New players should avoid this approach entirely**; consider it only after reaching Major (XP>100000) and following experienced players.

> **Note**: The enemy main base cannot be attacked until the hidden towers are destroyed. The base has heavy firepower, many deployable weapons, and its own signal jammers. Soldiers can carry five categories: **Primary**, **Secondary**, **Throwable**, **Armor/Clothing**, **Loot**. Hover over a soldier to see their current weapon and armor.

AI soldiers have classes; **players have no class** and can equip freely.

Both equipped slots and backpack can hold items. (Item operations see Basic Controls section)

<img src={images.img_040} style="max-width:100%;height:auto;" />

> **Soldier equipment and backpack slots**  
> Top to bottom: Primary, Secondary, Throwable, Armor/Clothing, Loot

### 5.1 Soldier Stats

Soldiers **do not have health pools**; instead they have **armor points**, **survivability**, and **stealth**.

#### ① Armor Points
Armor points indicate how many hits can be absorbed; **each layer absorbs one layer of damage**.

For example, Type II armor has 3 layers, absorbing 3 hits. When armor drops from high to low, the soldier will "flinch" (fall down but get up). When the last layer is destroyed, the soldier goes down.

<img src={images.img_041} style="max-width:100%;height:auto;" />

> **Type II armor**  
> Left: 1 layer left; Middle: 2 layers; Right: 3 layers

- **Without armor**, any damage kills instantly.
- **Multi‑layer damage** can penetrate low armor and kill, e.g., a grenade (2 layers) hitting 1‑layer armor kills.

> **Vulnerabilities**:
> - Some armors have bullet damage vulnerability at low/mid armor. E.g., **Bomb Suit**: mid‑armor resists explosion well but takes extra bullet damage (1 bullet hit may count as 2).
> - Some armors have explosion vulnerability at low armor. E.g., **Night Stealth Suit**: at 1 layer, a single explosion can kill; at full 2 layers, 2‑layer explosion kills due to layer‑by‑layer calculation.  
> (Layer calculation see 7‑1‑②)

**Different armors have special effects**:
- Higher armor points
- Anti‑flinch (only at high/mid armor)
- Increased survivability/stealth
- Melee immunity (only at high armor), instant‑down immunity (high armor)
- Some have negative effects like increased taunt, increased vulnerability at low/mid armor

> **Note**: Some armors are valuable, so avoid friendly fire.

In vanilla, **players respawn without armor**, AI may spawn with some. AI can have more armor layers than players for the same armor type; e.g., Bomb Suit gives AI 8 layers, players 6.

#### ② Survivability
Survivability is the **chance to avoid damage** when hit. Higher survivability means less chance to take damage.

It is determined by:
- Armor‑provided survivability
- Base soldier survivability

Some armors grant survivability bonuses. Players have low base survivability; AI have high. Enemy AI survivability also scales dynamically with the number of players.

> **Important**: All **bullet weapons** are affected by enemy survivability; **explosive weapons** ignore survivability and always deal damage.  
(Bullet vs explosive see 7‑1‑②)

#### ③ Stealth
Stealth **reduces the detection range** of the soldier. Higher stealth means enemies must be closer to spot you.

Stealth is affected by **stance** and armor bonuses.

- **Standing**: low stealth
- **Crouching**: medium
- **Prone**: highest

**Moving reduces stealth**; crouch‑moving or crawling maintains some stealth.

Some armors give stealth bonuses (camouflage, ghillie vests); others increase taunt (banana suit, clown suit).

> **Example**: Enemy snipers have long detection range; wearing camouflage, standing/running may be spotted, but crouch‑moving may not.

### 5.2 Soldier Stances

- **Standing**: fastest movement, low accuracy, low stealth
- **Crouching**: slower movement, higher accuracy, higher stealth

### 5.3 Soldier Status Effects

When hit, soldiers can be in three states: **Flinch**, **Downed**, or **Dead**.

#### ① Flinch
Temporary fall, will stand up shortly; usually triggered when armor is reduced to low.

> **⚠️ Special**: If flinched in water, you will **die instantly**! Be careful crossing rivers.

<img src={images.img_048} style="max-width:100%;height:auto;" />

> **Flinch state**

- Cannot act normally, but can open backpack (cannot manipulate items) until flinch ends.
- Dangerous to be flinched under enemy fire.

Flinch can also be triggered by explosions, flashbangs, banana peels, fuel barrels, etc., unless wearing anti‑flinch armor (high armor only). **The attack must leave enough armor layers to prevent death; otherwise you go down or die.**

#### ② Downed
Soldier is critically wounded, cannot get up; needs revival from a teammate. Cannot act, but can open backpack. **Only soldiers who had armor and lost their last layer** enter this state.

<img src={images.img_049} style="max-width:100%;height:auto;" />

> **Downed state**

- Can crawl while downed; enemies usually ignore downed soldiers, so crawl to safety.
- **Hold melee key to suicide**.
- Bleed out after time, losing XP and RP.

Downed can also be triggered by taser, blowgun, etc., unless wearing instant‑down immunity armor (high armor only).

#### ③ Dead
- Unarmored soldiers killed by any damage, or armored soldiers receiving more damage than remaining layers.
- Some armors have vulnerability that causes death at low/mid armor.

**Hostile and neutral damage** cause item drops.  
**Friendly fire** does not cause drops.  
(Damage types see 2‑3)

Suicide or bleeding out after downed also causes drops.

High‑speed vehicle collisions, drowning, etc. also kill. Disconnected players are not counted as dead.

## 6. AI Soldiers

### 6.1 AI Advantages and Disadvantages

**Advantages**:
- ① Higher weapon lethality, higher survivability, thicker armor.
- ② Enemy AI scales with player count, increasing survivability and spawn caps.
- ③ In vehicle or deployable weapon positions, they have better vision, accuracy, and lethality.
- ④ AI have aimbot; most weapons are more accurate than players.
- ⑤ Enemy AI have hidden spawn points, can ambush players.

## 7. Infantry Weapons

### 7.1 Basic Weapon Mechanics

#### ① Ammo and Firing
- All weapons **have unlimited ammo** – no need to resupply.
- Some weapons overheat after sustained fire.
- Some weapons require **crouching** or **prone / behind cover** to fire.

#### ② Damage Calculation and Types
- Damage to infantry armor is calculated **layer by layer**. E.g., 2 layers of damage against 3 layers: first apply 1 layer to 3, then 1 layer to the remaining 2.  
(Armor layers see 5‑1‑①)

**Damage types: Bullet / Explosive**

- **Bullet weapons**:
  - No damage to vehicles.
  - Against infantry armor: lethality between 0‑1 may cause 1 layer; higher value = higher chance. Lethality >1 causes at least 1 layer, with leftover chance for extra layers.  
  (Lethality see 7‑2‑①)

- **Explosive weapons**:
  - Against infantry: explosion damage is converted to lethality, **rounded up**, e.g., 100 damage = 1 lethality, 50 = 1, 105 = 2. Thus 50 and 80 damage both count as 1 layer; 100‑199 as 2 layers, etc.
  - Against vehicles: explosion deals its full damage value, affected only by vehicle armor. Damage below vehicle armor value is ineffective; e.g., Vulcan cannon vs main battle tank (high armor) is ineffective. Damage above armor value deals full damage.  
  (Vehicle armor see 8‑2‑①)

> **⚠️ Important**: Use explosive weapons with caution. **New players should avoid explosives** initially.

### 7.2 Key Weapon Stats

Important stats:
- Lethality
- Accuracy
- Recoil
- Recoil recovery
- Falloff range
- Maximum range
- Weight
- Vision

These interact with each other.

#### ① Lethality
Chance to **successfully deal damage** on hit. Each successful hit equals one layer of damage. Minimum 0 (no damage), maximum 1 (guaranteed 1 layer).

- All **explosive weapons** have highest lethality (rounded up) and ignore enemy survivability.  
(Explosive conversion see 7‑1‑②; Survivability see 5‑1‑②)

Damage success is affected by:
- **Positive**: weapon's lethality
- **Negative**: range falloff, enemy armor survivability, enemy base survivability

After combining, success is rolled.

> **Note**: Multi‑layer damage can penetrate low armor and kill, e.g., 2 layers vs 1 layer = instant death.

**Examples**:
- High lethality: sniper rifles, shotguns (close range)
- Multi‑layer: Barrett (lethality >1), rockets and grenades (high damage converted)
- Low lethality: PKM, MGV176 secondary SMG

#### ② Accuracy
Initial shot accuracy; absolute accuracy is 1.0 or above; lower value = worse spread.

Stance and backpack weight affect accuracy:
- **Moving**: significant accuracy penalty
- **Standing**: low
- **Crouching**: higher
- **Prone**: highest
- **Backpack weight ≥20%** begins to affect accuracy; heavier = more penalty.

> Some weapons may have different stance modifiers.

#### ③ Recoil
Amount of recoil per shot; higher recoil = larger spread.

Affected by stance:
- **Standing**: max
- **Crouching**: lower
- **Prone**: lowest

> Recoil **not affected by backpack weight**.

#### ④ Recoil Recovery
Speed at which recoil spread returns to normal; higher recovery = faster reset.

> Recoil recovery **not affected by stance or weight**.

#### ⑤ Falloff Range
Distance at which lethality begins to decrease. Longer falloff = lethality stays effective farther.

Beyond falloff range, lethality decreases with distance, down to 0.

> Explosive weapons **have no falloff**.

#### ⑥ Maximum Range
Distance at which lethality reaches zero. Longer max range = effective range extends farther.

Beyond max range, bullets deal no damage.

> Explosive weapons **have no max range**.

#### ⑦ Weight
Weapon weight; heavier = slower movement.

#### ⑧ Vision
Provides view distance; higher = see farther.

#### ⑨ Rate of Fire
How fast the weapon shoots.

#### ⑩ Bullet Velocity
Speed of projectile; higher = faster travel.

#### ⑪ Reload Style
- Most weapons use **magazine reload** – press reload to swap magazine.
- Some use **single‑round loading** – press reload to load one round.

## 8. Vehicles

### 8.1 Advice for New Players

> **⚠️ Strongly advised**:
> - Driving requires skill, map knowledge, route planning, and situational awareness. Be careful, avoid dangerous areas (front lines, water).
> - **New players should not drive attack vehicles (tanks, APCs) or support vehicles (respawn, armory, supply, comms, trucks)**.
> - Start with **transport vehicles (personnel carriers, jeeps, ATVs)** to gain experience. Ask veteran players for guidance; don't practice alone.
> - Recommended ranks: **Lieutenant Colonel (XP>20000)** before driving supply vehicles and lightly armed attack vehicles (Humvees/armed trucks).
> - **Major (XP>100000)** before driving trucks and attack vehicles (tanks, APCs).
> - **Lieutenant General (XP>200000)** before driving respawn and armory vehicles.
> - **Comms vehicles are not recommended for anyone**.

---

### 8.2 Vehicle Mechanics and Notes

#### ① HP and Armor
- Vehicles have HP; small vehicles low HP, medium moderate, large high. Use rockets/impact grenades/C4 or tank fire.
- Vehicles have armor values; most bullet weapons are ineffective.
- Explosive damage below vehicle armor is ineffective; e.g., Vulcan cannon vs MBT (high armor) does nothing. Damage above armor deals full damage.

#### ② Vehicle Weapon Ammo
- All vehicle weapons have **unlimited ammo**.
- Bullet/autocannon weapons **overheat**.
- Cannon weapons **auto‑reload** after each shot.

#### ③ Vehicle Seating
- Usually driver at front‑left, passenger/gunner at front‑right, rear seats for passengers/machine gun. Layout varies – explore in singleplayer.
- Some vehicles have **free seats** allowing use of personal primary/secondary/throwables.

#### ④ Vehicle Weight
- Heavier vehicles can push lighter ones. Small vehicles light, large heavy.
- **All vehicles are destroyed if flipped** – be careful with light/medium vehicles.
- High‑speed vehicles can damage sandbags/deployables/structures.

#### ⑤ Gene Lock / Vehicle Lock
- **Gene lock**: vehicle cannot be captured by other factions, even when empty (e.g., Radar Tank, Black Cat Tank).
- **Vehicle lock**: when an enemy is inside a seat, that seat is locked and cannot be taken over (e.g., NOXE missile vehicle, M528 support vehicle).

#### ⑥ Driving Tips
- Grey/yellow rocky areas slow vehicles and reduce steering – avoid if possible.
- Medium vehicles can crush small obstacles; large can crush large obstacles. Use to clear paths (see Chapter 10‑3).
- **All vehicles can crush infantry** – be careful not to run over allies; avoid using vehicles to crush enemies – it often loses the vehicle and crew.
- **Amphibious vs non‑amphibious**: Amphibious vehicles can traverse water; non‑amphibious will be destroyed if they enter water. Ships only work in water; on land they are destroyed.

## 9. Radio Support

### 9.1 Use and Limitations

#### ① Using Radio
Hold `H` (default) to open the support menu, click an option to call support. **The mouse cursor location before opening the menu is the target point**.

> AI also call support: standard soldiers call small‑scale, elites/lone wolves call medium/large, rangers call large.

#### ② Limitations
- Players must reach **Corporal (XP>1000)** to unlock radio access, starting with small support. Higher rank unlocks more options.
- Within enemy signal jammers (signal towers, jammer towers, comms vehicles), you cannot call support. Similarly, enemies are jammed by your jammers.  
  (Signal/jammer towers see 10‑1‑①; comms vehicle see 8‑3‑②)
- If your signal towers/comms vehicles are destroyed, you lose radio until they are restored. Same for enemies.
- If enemy **anti‑air guns** are present, you can **only call 1/2/5‑round artillery strikes**; no other air support.  
  (Anti‑air see 10‑1‑②)

## 10. Buildings

Most buildings are indestructible; destructible ones usually show HP (except fences, wire).

> **Hitbox**: the area around an object that can be damaged.

### 10.1 Military Structures

#### ① Signal Tower / Jammer Tower
- **Signal Tower**: provides radio access for its faction and jams enemy radio. Increases allied AI cap by 5‑10. Very high HP, best with C4, small hitbox, large jamming range.
  - When destroyed, faction loses radio, cannot see vehicles/buildings/deployables/spawn points on map, and AI lose attack orders.
- **Jammer Tower**: only jams enemy radio, no radio access. Increases AI cap by 5. Very high HP, best with C4, small hitbox, large jamming range. When destroyed, jamming effect ends.

> **⚠️ Note**: Signal/jammer towers are faction‑owned. **Identify friend from foe**!  
> **Maliciously destroying allied signal/jammer towers/comms vehicles may result in a ban.**  
> Signal towers are **triangular**, jammer towers are **quadrilateral**.

#### ② Anti‑Air Gun
Enemy‑only; prevents all allied air support (only artillery allowed). Increases enemy AI cap by 3. Very high HP, medium hitbox, global range. When destroyed, air support becomes available.

> Recommended to use **C4**. If unavailable, use multiple rockets/impact grenades.  
(Impact grenades see 7‑3‑②)

Enemy‑only:
- Prison Gate increases enemy AI cap by 5
- Prison Skylight increases enemy AI cap by 10

Very high HP, small hitbox, best with C4. Destroying them gives allied AI prisoner support.

### 10.2 Other Structures

#### ① Fuel Barrels
No function; explode when destroyed. Low HP, any explosion ignites, large hitbox. Explosion:
- Far: stun effect
- Close: stun + high damage
- Vehicles: minor damage

> Flashbangs and stun weapons also detonate them.

#### ② Fuel Tanks
No function; explode when destroyed. Moderate HP, 3 grenades or 1 rocket, large hitbox. Explosion:
- Far: stun + 1 layer damage
- Close: high damage
- Vehicles: high damage

Fuel tanks are faction‑owned; destroying enemy/neutral ones gives XP and RP. Allied fuel tank destroyed gives nothing.

#### ③ Water Towers
Faction‑owned; increases faction AI cap by 3. High HP, barely survives 3 rockets, best with C4, small hitbox. Destroying enemy/neutral gives XP and RP.

#### ④ Mortar Ammo Box
Increases faction AI cap by 3. Explodes when destroyed. Very low HP, 1 grenade, large hitbox. Explosion scatters mortar rounds around; same damage as mortar (high anti‑infantry, low vehicle).

Faction‑owned; destroying enemy gives XP and RP. Allied destroyed gives nothing.
